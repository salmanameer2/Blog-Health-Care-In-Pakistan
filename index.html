The Pulse of a Nation: Healthcare in Pakistan


In the heart of South Asia, Pakistan stands at a critical juncture in its healthcare journey. With a population exceeding 240 million, the country faces a complex web of challenges — from underfunded public hospitals to rising non-communicable diseases — yet also holds immense potential for transformation. This blog explores the state of healthcare in Pakistan, its systemic hurdles, emerging innovations, and the road ahead.


A Snapshot of the System


Pakistan’s healthcare system is a mix of public and private providers, with the government responsible for policy, regulation, and service delivery through federal and provincial health departments

Key Stats (2025 Estimates):

  • Health expenditure: ~3.1% of GDP
  • Doctor-to-patient ratio: ~1:1,300 (WHO recommends 1:1,000)
  • Hospital beds per 1,000 people: ~0.6
  • Infant mortality rate: ~54 per 1,000 live births
  • Life expectancy: ~67 years

Despite constitutional recognition of health as a basic right, access remains uneven — especially in rural areas, where 63% of the population resides.

Structure of the Healthcare System

  1. Public Sector

    • Managed by federal and provincial governments
    • Includes Basic Health Units (BHUs), Rural Health Centers (RHCs), Tehsil and District
    • Underfunding
    • Poor infrastructure and maintenance

  2. Private Sector

    • Accounts for over 70% of healthcare delivery
    • Includes private hospitals, clinics, diagnostic labs, and pharmacies
    • Offers better facilities and shorter wait times, but is expensive and largely unregulated

  • Infrastructure Deficiency

  • Rural areas lack access to basic healthcare
  • Urban hospitals are overcrowded and under-equipped
  • Many facilities lack clean water, sanitation, and electricity

  • Human Resource Shortage

  • Doctor-to-patient ratio is ~1:1300 (WHO recommends 1:1000)
  • Many healthcare professionals migrate abroad for better opportunities
  • Many facilities lack clean water, sanitation, and electricity
  • Gender disparities limit female participation in healthcare delivery, especially in rural areas

  • Diseases Burden

  • Communicable diseases: Polio, hepatitis B & C, tuberculosis, dengue
  • Non-communicable diseases: Diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, cancer
  • Mental health: Largely neglected due to stigma and lack of trained professionals

Recent Developments and Innovations

  • Sehat Sahulat Program

  • Government-backed health insurance for low-income families
  • Covers hospitalization, surgeries, maternity care, and more
  • Aims to reduce out-of-pocket expenses and improve access

  • Telemedicine and Digital Health

  • Platforms like Sehat Kahani, Oladoc, and Marham connect patients with doctors remotel
  • Especially beneficial for remote and underserved communities
  • COVID-19 accelerated adoption of virtual consultations

  • Immunization Campaigns

  • Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI) targets childhood diseases
  • Polio eradication efforts continue despite logistical and security challengess
  • Mobile vaccination units and awareness campaigns are improving coverage

The Road Ahead

Improving healthcare in Pakistan requires a multi-pronged approach:

  • Strengthen Primary Care: Focus on prevention and early intervention
  • ncrease Public Investment: Raise health spending to at least 5% of GDP
  • Train and Retain Workforce: Incentivize service in rural areas and reduce brain drain
  • Regulate Private Sector: Ensure quality, affordability, and ethical practices
  • Promote Health Literacy: Educate citizens on hygiene, nutrition, and preventive care
  • Integrate Mental Health: Normalize mental wellness and expand access to counseling

Human Stories Behind the Numbers

Behind every statistic is a story—a mother walking miles for prenatal care, a young doctor serving in flood-hit Sindh, or a nurse managing a rural clinic with limited supplies. These stories are the heartbeat of Pakistan’s healthcare journey and a reminder of the resilience and compassion that persist despite systemic challenges.

The Final Verdict

Pakistan’s healthcare system stands at a crossroads. With the right investments, reforms, and community engagement, it can evolve into a more inclusive and effective model. The journey is long, but the potential for transformation is real.
